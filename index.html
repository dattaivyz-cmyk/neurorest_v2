NeuroRest Prototype
NeuroRest · clickable prototype

A 45-second check before driving home from a night shift

Residents and nurses finishing night shifts and long calls take a short reaction-time check at the end of the shift. It compares them only to their own rested checks. If they come back off, one tap gets them the ride or the call-room nap their program already provides.

It never tells anyone they are safe to drive. It shows how this check compares to their usual, and the decision stays theirs.

  • Interns coming off shifts longer than 24 hours were more than twice as likely to crash driving home, and about five times as likely to have a near-miss. Barger et al., New England Journal of Medicine, 2005. 2,737 interns. Summary
  • On a closed track, 37.5% of night-shift workers had a near-crash driving after their shift. None did after a normal night's sleep. Lee et al., PNAS, 2015. 16 participants. Summary
  • Programs must provide safe transportation for residents who may be too fatigued to drive home, and sleep facilities. ACGME Institutional Requirements, 2026, 3.2.g.5.b and 3.2.g.5.c. PDF
  1. Tap Check before driving and do the 15-tap check, or use the sample post-shift result.
  2. Choose a ride home, a call-room nap and a second check, or driving.
  3. Look through History, Method, and the schedule scan on the Shift tab.

Prototype with sample data. Nothing you do here is saved or sent. Reaction times in a browser are approximate; the iPhone app times taps on touch-down. In the app, all data stays on the phone and there is no supervisor view.

Method

How the check works, and what it can't tell you

What it measures

A short version of the psychomotor vigilance task, the reaction-time test sleep researchers use. A dot appears at random moments and you tap as fast as you can, 15 times. NeuroRest counts responses slower than half a second.

Compared only to you

Phones add their own delay and people differ, so there are no population cutoffs. Your usual is learned from your own checks, ideally at the start of shifts when you're rested.

What it can't tell you

It can't tell you whether you're safe to drive. No phone test has been shown to predict crash risk, so NeuroRest never says you're fine. It is shorter than the validated 3-minute version, so single results are rough. It is not a medical device.

Privacy

Everything stays on your phone. There's no account and no supervisor view. The only thing that ever leaves the phone is a call or text you choose to make.
